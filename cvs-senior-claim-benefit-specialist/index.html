03

Must-Know Information

Know these details before you apply or interview.

01

This is a senior adjudication role

You may receive claims that exceed another processor's authority or technical expertise. Be prepared to explain how you research, validate, calculate, and document a defensible outcome.

02

Production and accuracy are equally important

High claim volume will not outweigh poor payment accuracy. Strong candidates discuss both throughput and quality metrics, including audit scores, turnaround time, and rework rates.

03

Medical necessity is not a guess

Coverage decisions must be tied to the plan, policy, clinical criteria, documentation, authorization requirements, and applicable regulations—not personal opinion.

04

Payment irregularities require escalation

Overpayments, underpayments, duplicate claims, coding conflicts, eligibility discrepancies, and configuration concerns must be documented and routed through the appropriate process.

05

Self-funded plans may follow unique rules

Employer-sponsored self-funded plans can include customized benefit language, exclusions, accumulators, and administrative guidelines. Preferred experience here can strengthen your candidacy.

06

Outbound calls are part of claims resolution

You may contact providers, facilities, members, or internal partners to obtain missing information for initial claims or reconsideration requests.

Interview Blueprint™

Prepare for the technical and behavioral questions.

Your 60-second introduction

“I have more than five years of medical claims experience with health insurance payers and third-party administrators. In my current role, I review 150 to 180 complex claims per day while maintaining a quality score above 98%. My experience includes eligibility and benefit verification, medical necessity review, claim adjustments, reconsiderations, payment-integrity research, and coaching less-experienced processors. I am interested in this CVS Health role because it combines complex adjudication with technical leadership and member-focused issue resolution.”

Strong structure: Confirm member and service details → verify eligibility and plan coverage → review coding, network status, authorization, and medical necessity → check coordination of benefits and accumulators → calculate the allowable and member responsibility → document and finalize or escalate.

Use STAR. Focus on why the claim was complex, the sources you reviewed, the conflicting information you reconciled, the decision you made, and the measurable impact. Do not share protected health information.

Discuss queue prioritization, standardized review steps, work batching, quality self-checks, policy references, and when you slow down or escalate instead of forcing an uncertain decision.

Explain what triggered your concern, how you recalculated the expected outcome, which documentation or contract terms you used, how you reported it, and the final correction or savings.

Show how you diagnosed the knowledge gap, explained the rule, used examples, checked understanding, and tracked improvement. Senior-level coaching should build independence.

Do not override casually. Validate the member, dates, configuration, plan language, and processing history; preserve documentation; follow authority limits; and escalate potential configuration or systemic issues through the approved channel.

Questions to ask the interviewer

  • What types of claims most often require senior specialist review?
  • How are production and quality measured during and after training?
  • What distinguishes a strong performer in the first 90 days?
  • How frequently does this role coach peers or handle formal escalations?
  • How is DG used within the claims workflow?

Recruiter Secrets™

Small positioning choices that strengthen your application.

01

Lead with payer-side experience

The posting specifically requires health insurance payer or third-party administrator claims processing.

02

Quantify claim volume and quality

Numbers prove you can succeed in a production-driven environment without sacrificing accuracy.

03

Show senior-level authority

Mention escalations, complex claim types, technical research, claim values, and decisions beyond basic processing.

04

Use “adjudicated,” not only “processed”

Adjudication signals that you evaluated eligibility, coverage, policy, coding, and payment—not merely data entry.

05

Address the preferred qualifications

Place self-funded plan experience and DG knowledge prominently when accurate.

06

Include coaching results

State who you supported and what improved: quality, productivity, confidence, or escalation reduction.
